MCP authorization enforces scopes on HTTP, as `spec/authorization.md` M6 specifies. A command declares the OAuth scopes a caller's token needs with `scopes` (`defineCommand({ scopes })`, and `CommandDefinition.scopes` in `@lushly-dev/afd-core`), reported as `_meta.scopes` and by `afd-detail`; stdio, the CLI, `DirectClient` and in-process registries ignore it. `createMcpHandler({ auth })` takes `requiredScopes` (needed by every request), a `resolver` (a `ScopeResolver` deciding each call from the command, its input and the credentials, which may answer `'defer'`) and `filterToolsByScope`, which the previous release refused.

A missing scope is one 403 before dispatch, with M5's exact `WWW-Authenticate` challenge and body naming every missing scope of every step once, in step order: for a `tools/call` on `/mcp` and `/message` (a command's own tool, a grouped action, `afd-call`, every step of `afd-batch` and `afd-pipe`), and for `/rpc`, `/batch` and `/stream`. A JSON-RPC batch is refused with 400 first, and every other message needs the required scopes. A call decided after routing fails with `INSUFFICIENT_SCOPE` (`retryable: false`, `details.scopes`) before the confirmation gate, a batch or pipeline as a whole; with a resolver, every command is decided again at execution on its validated input, defaults applied and pipeline references resolved. With `filterToolsByScope`, the listings show only what the caller may use, `tools/list` then carries `cacheScope: "private"`, and with a resolver a command its `known` rejects does not exist for the caller on any route: it is answered exactly as a name that does not exist, and no confirmation, suggestion or `requires` names it.

A host whose own layer vouches for its callers (a gateway that authenticated them itself) admits their credentials with `handler.admit(req, credentials)`, checked, frozen and branded as verified ones, and runs the bearer layer's own decision before dispatch with `handler.preDispatchCheck({ challenge, scopeSource }).decide(req, body)`, which answers the same 403, 400 or 413, a 403 in the host's own scheme (`ScopeChallenge`) when it names one; the handler then takes the body it read as its third argument. `admit` exists only on the handler object. The exported `staticDecision`, `requestDecision` and `ScopeChallenge` are the decisions' pure parts.
