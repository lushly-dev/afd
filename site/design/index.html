Agent-First Design

The design side of Agent-First Development

Agents are users too.

Agent-First Design is designing a product for two users: the person who asks and the agent that acts. It is the second half of Agent-First Development, an open-source way of building software as commands that people and agents both call. It is about software agents can use, not agents writing your code.

Design answers for the experience. That used to mean one kind of user. Now a person asks, an agent acts, and both have to find their way through the same product.

01 / The person's side

Intent and authority.

Saying what you want, understanding what was done, deciding what goes ahead and stepping in when it shouldn't.

02 / The agent's side

Legible capability.

Actions it can discover, inputs it can supply, results it can predict and failures it can act on.

03 / The journey they share

One story.

The same identity, state and meaning for both, with the same permission and the same way back, whoever is acting.

What can I do? What comes back? Should this go ahead? What now? Interfaces have answered these for people for decades. An agent needs the same answers, in a form it can read.

Fig. 05The character

A mark that
looks back.

Alfred is the agent's face across AFD. At rest he is a compass: one bar in a ring, leaning like the slash, turning slowly to find you. Come close and he wakes.

Two bars, one angle.
The lean of the wordmark slash never changes.
Two moves.
A bar slides along its axis or changes length. Nothing bends or grows a mouth.
He rests as the mark.
The face appears when he has something to do, then leaves.
A designer's perspective
